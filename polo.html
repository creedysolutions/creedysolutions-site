Open Polo next to Setup and it follows whatever you click. No copy-pasting API names, no second window, no two Setup tabs side by side and squinting.

VERDICT

The line answers first

A straight line means the two orgs match. A branch means one of them moved, and it shows you which one and when. Every change reads as a plain sentence: “required: false → true”, “picklist value missing in target: Negotiating”, with the full diff one click away.

WHO

Who touched it last, in each org

One line per org with the person and the date. Full history merges the Setup Audit Trail and flow versions into one timeline for both orgs, live from Salesforce.

DEEPER

Field access, permission sets, flow versions

Field access shows which profiles and permission sets can read or edit a field in each org: the “works in sandbox, broken in prod” answer a metadata diff can't give. Compare a permission set's access, scan a whole object, or diff two versions of a flow.

HAND-OFF

Something to attach to the ticket

Copy for ticket puts the facts on your clipboard for Jira, Slack or the PR: what differs, where, who changed it last. Report opens a print-ready page of the diff and history.

Org drift: every difference, on one line.

Pick two orgs and Polo lists both, compares everything they share, and draws the result. Everything that matches is one glowing line. Every component that drifted branches off on the day it changed and runs to today: above the line if the target org moved, below if the source did.

  • Dive into any branch. The card shows both orgs' values side by side, who changed it in each org, and its history. Arrow keys step through the rest.
  • Together, by type or by object. One line for everything, a line per metadata type, or a line per object: everything that drifted on Quote, Opportunity, your custom objects.
  • No noise you didn't ask for. Managed packages and untouched sandbox copies are skipped by default, one click brings them back.
  • Opens instantly the second time. Scans are saved in your browser. Reopen the map and Polo re-checks only what moved since. It even starts the scan in the background when you open the side panel.

Quick, Full or Everything. You pick the depth.

Real orgs have thousands of components. Speed matters, and so does knowing what you're looking at. One switch decides how hard Polo looks.

QUICK

Seconds, not minutes

A few bulk queries per type. Exact for Apex, Visualforce, Lightning components and custom labels. It compares content, never deploy dates, so a pipeline that re-deploys the same code doesn't light up the map. What Quick can't settle, it says so instead of guessing.

FULL

The whole story

Reads every changed component's full definition: fields, flows, layouts, rules, pages and the rest. Slower, and it's the one to run before a release.

EVERYTHING

About 50 more types

Full, plus what only the Metadata API can read: sharing rules, approval processes, org settings, translations, Agentforce. It needs “Modify Metadata” on your user, so it's opt-in.

Recent changes: who changed what, and did it make it?

Every component changed in an org since a date you pick, grouped by type or by person, and checked live against the next org in your pipeline.

PACKAGE.XML

A manifest, one click

Tick the changes that are ready and Polo writes a Metadata API manifest for exactly those components. Retrieve from source, deploy to target, with your normal tooling and your normal approvals.

WATCH

Pin what's in flight

Add anything to the watchlist and re-check it in one click. Turn on the daily re-check and Polo badges its toolbar icon when something drifts. The map can show just your watchlist, too.

No OAuth, no connected apps, no new passkeys. Any two orgs.

Polo rides on the Salesforce sessions you already have in Chrome. That turns out to matter more than it sounds.

SIGN IN LIKE ALWAYS

Nothing to install in the org

No connected app, no OAuth dance, no package, no API user to provision. Log into Salesforce in Chrome the way you already do and every org you're signed into shows up in Polo's org picker.

UNRELATED ORGS

They don't have to be a sandbox and its prod

Polo matches components by API name, never by ID, so the two orgs don't need to be related at all. Two customers on different stacks, an old org and the one replacing it, a client's prod against your reference org: if you can log into both, you can compare them.

READ-ONLY, LOCAL

Look, don't touch

Polo only ever reads. Metadata goes from your orgs straight to your browser and stays there: no servers, no accounts, no telemetry.

Read-only isn't a setting. It's the product.

Polo never writes to an org, and your metadata never leaves your browser.

  • No deploys, ever. Polo writes a package.xml, you run the retrieve or deploy. Its network layer is hard-wired to read. The one exception is the Metadata API's list and read calls, which Salesforce only offers as POST requests; Polo can't send any other kind.
  • No servers, no accounts. The only calls are from your browser to your own orgs' APIs, using the sessions you already have.
  • No secrets in your clipboard. Connected app keys and secrets are dropped before anything is saved, compared or copied.
  • No telemetry. We don't know when you install it, open it, or what orgs you point it at.
  • Nothing installed in your org. No connected app, no OAuth, no package.
