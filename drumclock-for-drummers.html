DrumClock from the drum throne: where the phone goes, the count-off, and what you get out of it

First, who's writing this: I'm not a drummer. I'm the guitar player who has stood six feet from one for thirty-five years, so everything here about feel is what I've watched, not what I've felt. The drummers I've handed this to pushed back on the same thing, so let's start there.

It follows you. It doesn't grade you.

A click points at you. You follow it, and if you push the chorus, you're wrong. DrumClock points the other way. It listens to the kick and snare, works out your tempo, and sends that tempo to whatever in the band needs one: the keys player's delays and arps, the guitar player's dotted eighths, a sequencer, Ableton. When you push the chorus, their gear pushes with you. Nobody's wrong, because you're the reference.

The short version: the band's effects stop fighting you and start agreeing with you, and nobody has to put you in in-ears to make that happen.

What it listens for

Mostly the kick and the snare. They have a shape, a fast hit that decays, and the cymbals are a wash it can look past. The current version follows hit to hit, so if you lean into a chorus the clock is there on the next beat. There's a Smooth setting in Outputs if a band wants it to ease instead; Precise is the default because the drummers who tried it said they wanted it exactly where they were.

Where the phone goes

  • In the cage: best. A clamp low near the kick. It hears the kick first and the PA is mostly behind it.
  • No cage: a music stand beside the hi-hat, pointed at the kit, with the bass amp not pointed at it.
  • Mic'd stage: skip the phone mic. Put the kick and snare through a USB audio interface into the phone or iPad and it hears what the board hears. The Scarlett guide covers that wiring.
  • iPad on a stand: my favourite. A big ring that pulses on the beat, the number next to it, and a view that's just a ball going side to side, so you can see it from the throne without reading anything. Most drummers look at it for one song and then stop looking, which is the right outcome.

The count-off is yours

Click the sticks three or four times, the way you already do. DrumClock takes the tempo from the sticks, so by beat one the clock is already running at your count and the keys player's delay lands on the first downbeat. Nobody tapped anything.

Breaks and rubato

When you stop, it holds the last tempo until you come back in, then picks you up again. For a rubato bridge, put it on Hold on purpose and let it sit until the band is back on the grid. The first version chased the pad during a break and that was a bad night, so this got fixed early.

Presets are a guard rail, not a leash

Build the set list in the app so every song has a starting tempo. Two things happen. The band can see the song's tempo before you count it, which ends the "was that too fast" conversation. And the app uses that number as an anchor: it follows you inside about eight BPM of it, so a big fill or a double-time hat doesn't throw it into another tempo. Page through the set with a Bluetooth foot switch or keyboard; on iPad the space bar and arrow keys do it.

The hard cases, honestly

  • Brushes: hard. There isn't much of a hit to grab. Try it, don't count on it.
  • Quiet jazz kit, tiny kick: works if the phone is close. Get it nearer the snare.
  • Cajon: the slap is a clean transient, so it generally tracks well. Place it close.
  • Loud stage, subs by the kit: placement. Closer to the kick than anything else, gain down until the kick is the biggest thing it hears, or go through the kick mic and an interface.

What the drummer actually gets

The keys player gets delays on the beat and the guitar player gets repeats on the beat, so what's in it for the person being listened to? Nobody asks you to wear in-ears and play to a click so the rest of the band's gear works. You play the room the way the room needs it, push when it needs pushing, sit back on the bridge, and the effects come with you. And the band stops blaming the drummer for the delay being off, because the delay is on the drummer now, by definition.

Try it for one rehearsal

Put it in the cage, don't tell the band what it is, and look down at the ball a couple of times. See if the number is where you thought you were.
