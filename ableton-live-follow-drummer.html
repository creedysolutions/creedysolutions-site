Make Ableton Live follow a live drummer: Tempo Follower vs MIDI clock from the drums

The usual Ableton rig on a stage makes Live the master: the set runs at a fixed tempo, the drummer wears in-ears and plays to the click, and the band follows the drummer. That is fine for a band that wants to play that way. It falls apart the moment the drummer doesn't have in-ears, or the band wants the groove to breathe, or the one song that speeds up in the last chorus is the whole point of the song.

The alternative is to make Live follow the drummer. There are two ways to do it, and they're not the same.

Option 1: Live's Tempo Follower

Live 11 added a Tempo Follower. It listens to an audio input, works out the tempo, and nudges the set tempo to match. Setup:

  1. Get the drums into the laptop's interface: a kick and snare mic, a drum overhead, or an aux send from the desk.
  2. Preferences, Link / Tempo / MIDI: set the Tempo Follower input to that channel.
  3. Turn on Follow in the Control Bar (it appears once an input is chosen).

What works: it's built in, it's free, and loops, arps and warped clips follow the drummer without any extra hardware.

What doesn't: the laptop has to hear the drums, so there's a cable from the stage to the interface that didn't exist before. Live is still the master, so pedals and keys rigs that want tempo have to take it from Live as MIDI clock out, which means every tempo-aware device on the stage runs through the laptop. And if Live isn't in the rig that night, nothing follows anybody.

Option 2: MIDI clock from a tracker by the kit

The other approach puts a small beat tracker next to the drums and makes that the master. It listens to the kit, finds the beat, and sends MIDI clock. Live syncs to it the same way it would sync to any external clock:

  1. Connect the tracker to the laptop as MIDI: USB, a 5-pin interface, or Network MIDI over Ethernet.
  2. Preferences, Link / Tempo / MIDI, MIDI Ports: turn on Sync for the tracker's input port.
  3. Press EXT in the Control Bar. Live's tempo now comes from the clock.

What works: no audio into the laptop. The same clock can go to the keys rig and to the delay pedals at the same time, so the whole stage shares one beat and none of it depends on the laptop. Tap-tempo CC for gear that can't read clock (Sunday Keys, some delays) comes off the same device.

What doesn't: it's another box on the stage, and Live under external sync behaves differently from Live as master. Warped audio follows, but Live's own tempo automation is ignored while EXT is on, and some people see a little more timing wander than they get from Link. Use a wired MIDI transport for clock; Bluetooth is fine for tap tempo and rehearsal but jitters on clock.

Both at once

If Live is already the hub of your rig, there's nothing wrong with running the tracker into Live and letting Live pass clock to everything else. You get the "no audio into the laptop" part and the "one master" part. The tradeoff is that the laptop is back in the critical path.

None of this replaces a click when you're running fixed backing tracks. Audio that can't move has to be the master. Tempo following is for bands where the drummer leads and the tracks are loops, arps and effects that can follow them.

Which one

  • Live is the only tempo-aware thing on stage and you already have drums on an interface channel: Tempo Follower.
  • Keys rig, pedals, and Live all want tempo, or Live isn't there every night: a tracker by the kit sending MIDI clock.
  • Keys player on an iPad with no laptop at all: the tracker, straight to the keys app, Live not involved.
